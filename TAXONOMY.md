# Atlas — style taxonomy

A grouping of the 29 style pages into named clusters, for later use in homepage
filtering and card sequencing. This is a planning/documentation artefact only —
no `index.html` or `styles/*.html` file changes with it. Every page keeps its
existing position and content; a cluster is metadata about the set, not
something any page currently declares about itself.

Built by reading each page's Era/Origin field (in its Design Points block) and
general visual character, then grouping by what actually organizes the style —
not by surface similarity alone. Two of the pages don't have an obvious home in
the most literal reading of the source material; both are placed below with the
reasoning spelled out, and one page (Ethereal) is flagged as the softest fit in
its cluster rather than force-fit somewhere cleaner-looking.

## Clusters

### 1. Foundational
**Minimalism · Swiss Design · Brutalism · Neo-Brutalism**

Reduction and structural honesty as the organizing principle, arrived at from
two directions: restraint (Swiss's International Typographic Style grid
discipline, Minimalism's descendant SaaS reading of it) and exposed rawness
(Brutalism's raw-material architecture ethic applied to the web, Neo-Brutalism
borrowing that rawness back as a decorative language rather than an ideology).
All four treat structure itself, not surface decoration, as the content.

### 2. Surface Materials
**Glassmorphism · Neumorphism · Claymorphism**

Three consecutive early-2020s product-design trends (2019–2022, each named
within a year or two of the last) that share one move: simulate a physical
surface material — frosted glass, soft single-colour emboss, inflated pastel
clay — entirely through shadow and light, without a real material behind it.

### 3. Product Systems
**Bento Grid · Dark Mode UI · Anthropomorphic**

The user's draft grouped this trio into "Contemporary UI" together with the
three Surface Materials styles above. Splitting them out: these three aren't
simulating a surface, they're each a *system*-level product decision —
layout (Bento's compartmentalized tiles), theming (Dark Mode's token-driven
surfaces, not a material metaphor), and brand personality (Anthropomorphic's
character-led components, per its own Era/Origin field: "brand systems built
on a personality rather than a logo"). Anthropomorphic was one of the two
styles the starting draft left unplaced; it belongs here rather than with the
surface-simulation trio because Clippy, Freddie and Duo are interface/brand
systems, not a rendering technique.

### 4. Period Revival
**Neo-classical · Victorian · Gothic · Wabi Sabi**

The starting draft called this cluster "Historical/Ornate," which fits three
of the four (Neo-classical's gilt restraint spent only on ornament, Victorian's
damask and gilt double rules, Gothic's stained glass and blackletter) but not
Wabi Sabi, whose entire premise is the *absence* of ornament — asymmetry,
emptiness, the weathered and incomplete. Renamed to "Period Revival": what
actually unifies these four is that each reconstructs one specific historical
period's real visual grammar and material craft — Gothic's 12th–16th-century
cathedral stonework and blackletter hands, Neo-classical's post-Pompeii order,
Victorian's 1837–1901 jobbing print trade, Wabi Sabi's Momoyama-era tea
aesthetic under Sen no Rikyū — rather than a generic "old-fashioned" gesture.
Ornate or restrained is a second axis within the cluster, not the throughline.

### 5. Retro-Tech Revival
**Cyberpunk · Cybercore · Synthwave · Y2K Aesthetic · Pixel Art**

The starting draft's "Neon/Digital" (Cyberpunk, Cybercore, Synthwave, Y2K)
already shared a throughline: each reconstructs a specific past era's
technology-constrained visual language, revived now as a deliberate nostalgia
aesthetic rather than a contemporary one. Pixel Art — the draft's other
unplaced style — fits that same throughline exactly (1977–1995 console/arcade
hardware constraints, "revived deliberately by indie games from the late
2000s," per its own Era/Origin field) even though its palette isn't neon.
Renamed from "Neon/Digital" to "Retro-Tech Revival" so the cluster's name
matches its actual organizing idea rather than one member's colour palette,
which comfortably seats Pixel Art as a fifth member alongside the original four.

### 6. Expressive/Raw
**Maximalism · Surrealism · Graffiti · Scrapbook · Mixed Media**

Kept as drafted. Deliberate collision is the organizing device across all
five, just achieved by different means: Maximalism stacks colour and shadow,
Surrealism collides plausible rendering with impossible relationships,
Graffiti layers aerosol outline/overspray/drip, and Scrapbook and Mixed Media
both collide physical reproduction processes on one sheet (torn kraft board
and washi tape; halftone, riso overprint and photocopy grain). Density and
energy over restraint is what these five have in common.

### 7. Editorial/Craft
**Editorial Design · Luxury Typography · Conceptual Sketch · Bohemian · Ethereal**

Kept as drafted, with one flag. Editorial Design and Luxury Typography are
both explicit print/typography disciplines (magazine art direction, fine
maison typesetting); Conceptual Sketch and Bohemian are both explicit craft
processes (technical drafting, textile/block-print/macramé-making) rather
than decorative trends. Ethereal is the softest fit here: its own Era/Origin
field says outright it is "not a historical movement but a contemporary
mood," and its signature move (no containers, aurora veils, drifting motes)
is atmosphere rather than a craft or print discipline. It doesn't belong in
any other cluster more than this one — nothing else among the 29 shares its
quiet, unstructured register — but it's held here by shared *tone* (small
type, restraint, nothing loud) rather than by the craft/discipline throughline
that actually unifies the other four. Worth knowing before this cluster is
used to imply "these five are all about craft process," which is only true
of four of them.

## Deviations from the starting draft, summarized

- **Split** "Contemporary UI" (6 styles) into **Surface Materials** (3) and
  **Product Systems** (3) — a real difference in kind (rendering technique vs.
  system-level product decision), not just a headcount fix.
- **Renamed** "Historical/Ornate" → **Period Revival** so Wabi Sabi's
  restraint-first character isn't misdescribed by "Ornate."
- **Renamed** "Neon/Digital" → **Retro-Tech Revival** and added **Pixel Art**
  as a fifth member — it shares the cluster's actual throughline (a specific
  past era's tech-constrained language, deliberately revived) even though its
  palette isn't neon.
- **Anthropomorphic** placed in the new Product Systems cluster.
- **Ethereal** kept in Editorial/Craft as drafted, flagged as the one soft fit.
